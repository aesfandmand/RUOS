# Card anatomy references — sent 2026-10-13

Eleven card and panel studies. §12 governs the compact mobile card and §13 the
wide card; this set settles two things those sections left open.

## 1. The same content, two card shapes

`04-two-card-variants-overlay-vs-body` and `11-same-content-two-card-shapes`
both show one subject built twice:

- **Overlay** — the photo fills the card, the title, meta and action sit over a
  bottom scrim.
- **Body** — the photo is the top two-thirds, everything else sits on white
  below it, with the action as a pill at the end of a stat line.

This is the choice §13 asks for, shown as a pair. Pick one per row; never both
in the same row. `10-overlay-cards-tags-and-pill` is the overlay treatment in a
three-up, with tag chips under the description and a white pill action.

## 2. Glass panels

`01`, `02`, `03`, `05`, `06` are blurred panels over a photo or a gradient.

**Where this is allowed here:** over a photograph only — a hero, a structure
shot, a workshop image. It is the same permission §13 already gives a dark
scrim over a photo, and for the same reason: the ground is the photograph, not
a dark section.

**Where it is not:** a glass panel floating on `--paper` or white is a panel
with a grey tint and a border. It buys nothing and it costs a backdrop filter.

Legibility is the gate. Text over glass needs a measured contrast ratio against
the actual photograph behind it, at both breakpoints — the blur does not
guarantee it, and a pale sky will fail where a dark structure passes. Measure
it; do not assume it.

`08-goal-card-progress-split` is the one card here with a two-ground body —
white above, a coloured panel below carrying a progress bar. Useful for a
project or campaign status card.

## 3. Menus

`05`, `06`, `07` are icon rail + expanded panel. **Our navigation is locked**
(CLAUDE.md §1). These are filed for future in-page or dashboard panels, not for
the site header or the drawer. Nothing in this folder may move those files.

`09-3d-app-ui-screens` is a Pinterest capture of a 3D-rendered app UI — kept
for the rendering style of the objects, not the layout.
